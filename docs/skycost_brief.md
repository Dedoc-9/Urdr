# `skycost` (URDRSKY1) — design brief

*The far field's price, sealed; and the sky label checked against bytes.*

## Observe

v1.12 composed the far-field channel behind the terrain under the pixel-identity contract,
verified on the authoring container: zero terrain-owned pixels moved across the committed
walk at three reaches, the sky non-vacuous, sky-on replay deterministic, the default
untouched. The named host then priced the feature the way every visual feature is priced —
the same committed walk at the frozen competitive defaults, once with the sky off and once
with the starfield on. The off-run's chain equaled the committed reach-60 oracle exactly;
the on-run's chain equaled the container's sky-on chain digest for digest — cross-OS
byte-identity for the composed sky, before any price was read.

## Orient

Three claims wanted enforcement. The LABEL is not a field: a record saying `sky off` must
CARRY the oracle chain, and a record saying `starfield` must carry the committed container
sky chain and differ from the oracle — so a mislabeled record is refused by its own bytes,
and "the default leaves every sealed chain standing" is re-proven on every gate run rather
than remembered from a delivery note. The FREEZE is not prose: both records must wear the
frozen configuration's signature — reach 60, the ladder re-derived from the v2 model,
prefill equal to the ladder's own footprint, the cache cap equal to exactly twice it with
the derived-rail policy line and zero evictions. And the PRICE is not a quote: per-segment
median deltas derive from the sealed bytes, must all be positive (a free sky painted
nothing), and the verdict classifies with pixelcost's semantics against the 8.33 ms slot.

## Decide

The sealed evidence licenses one sentence: on the committed walk at the frozen competitive
defaults, the starfield costs roughly half a millisecond of median raster per segment
(465..717 microseconds), lifts the ceiling from 6.67 to 6.72 ms against the 8.33 ms slot,
and leaves zero frames late — the far field rides INSIDE the competitive 120 Hz profile.
What it does not license: the sky's price at reach 120 or 1080p (unswept), the sky's look
(recorded prose), or the decision to default the sky on — that is the operator's, made the
freeze pattern's way.

## Act

`skycost-records` re-reads the three pins, checks both labels against their bytes and both
records against the freeze signature; `skycost-verdict` derives the price and holds the
competitive-profile classification with the pinned scene; `skycost-selftest` proves six
plants bite. The falsifier naming this brief: relabel the off-record `starfield`, or edit
one digest in the on-record, and `skycost-verdict`'s admission refuses before any price is
spoken.
