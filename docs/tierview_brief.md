# `tierview` — design brief (URDRTIR1, S6, visual asymmetry zero by construction)

## What it is

**Making pay-to-win impossible rather than merely bounded.** If clients can buy higher quality tiers,
the question is whether a better tier ever lets you *see something an opponent cannot*. The usual
answer is a budget: bound the asymmetry and audit it. This rung answers structurally instead.

## The core law (what `tierview-law` certifies)

**The authoritative visibility predicate takes NO TIER ARGUMENT — so the asymmetry between quality
tiers is ZERO BY CONSTRUCTION rather than bounded by a budget.** Decided over every observer on both
pinned walls and every ordered tier pair, and **the decoupling is STRUCTURAL rather than
disciplinary**: the predicate cannot read a tier because it cannot receive one. There is no policy to
enforce and no budget to audit, because the input the exploit would need never reaches the function.

`tierview-selftest` earns the zero rather than asserting it. **A tier-reading authority path costs
1152 cells of asymmetry across the very census the law scores zero on** — so the zero is a result, not
an artifact of a census too weak to show anything. And that path is **REFUSED rather than warned
about**, on a stated ground: a tier pair resolving different cells is *an unequal game*, not a cosmetic
difference. The handed-down luminance measure is refuted in the same row.

## The seam (P61's finding)

**A zero earned by a plant that could have made it nonzero — the arc's recurring standard, here at its
sharpest.** `sea-marangoni` insisted a conserved quantity be tested by a defect that preserves it;
`splitview` showed a 0-of-240 that is a property of the input; this module shows 1152 cells of
asymmetry available to a predicate that merely *accepts a tier argument*. The gap between 1152 and 0
is the whole content, and neither number means anything without the other.

The structural-versus-disciplinary distinction is the transferable part. A bounded asymmetry needs an
enforcement mechanism, an auditor, and a story about what happens when the bound is exceeded. A
predicate that cannot take a tier needs none of those, and the "policy" question disappears rather than
being answered. That is the same move `autoroute` makes with capability projection and `provbind` makes
by recomputing at serve time: **deny the input rather than police the use.**

## does_not_show

Tiers outside the pinned walls and observer census; that quality tiers are otherwise FAIR (this is
about visibility asymmetry alone — latency, bandwidth and client capability are untouched); the
rendering pipeline downstream of the predicate; that no *other* channel leaks tier information. Zero
asymmetry in the authoritative predicate is not zero advantage in a real game. `integrity ≠ truth`.
