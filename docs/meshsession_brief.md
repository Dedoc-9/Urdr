# `meshsession` — design brief (URDRMSS1, M5, the attested mesh session)

**Read**: 2026-08-05, the READ pass under the lex successor selector — P48 of batch 14
(`../exe_epistemics/PREDICTIONS.md`). Outcome: **C-AB** — the **ROLE**-reading, which **falsifies
FP-ROW's directional claim** by its own frozen terms. Reading grade: **CONFIRMATION**.

## What it is

**Phase M's capstone: everything the mesh proved separately, run as one session.** `nway` certified
concurrency, `migrate` certified authority transfer, `partition` certified the CP posture, and
`meshattest` showed the migration law surviving real sockets. Each was certified in isolation. This
rung plays whole multi-authority campaigns and asks whether the laws still hold *together*, over a
timeline long enough for their interactions to matter.

## The core law (what `meshsession:sessions` certifies)

**Concurrency (M1), migration (M2) and a partition episode (M4) COMPOSED into one attested timeline** —
the campaign and skirmish multi-authority playthroughs reproducing URDRMSS1 checkpoint-chain digests,
**deterministically**. The distinctive claim is the composition: not that each law holds, which its own
rung established, but that a session exercising all three in sequence produces one chain no member
contradicts.

`meshsession:forges` supplies the adversarial half under the same principle `meshattest` established —
**reality may not overrule the COMPOSED mesh law on any axis of the session**: a tampered tick witness,
a tampered partition witness, a forged custody head, a dropped episode, and a bumped admitted count
each refuse. `meshsession-selftest` shows a single byte flip in the pinned session trace refusing on
the self-digest.

## The seam (P48's finding, and it cost a prediction)

**A capstone composes, and composition is the law — which is what the ROLE line said.** FP-ROW
pre-declared a role-reading of C-AB (a capstone composes previously-certified laws, the
`panelight`/`panewire` shape) against a row-reading of C-EQ (Phase M had resolved C-EQ twice running,
at `mesh` and `meshattest`). The row-reading lost. Recorded plainly: the five-batch pattern that
motivated FP-ROW does **not** generalize into "the row always wins", and one joint where the role prose
was the better guide is enough to falsify a directional claim stated that strongly.

An honest complication belongs here rather than in a footnote. The frozen fallback ladder selects the
first substantive row in source order, excluding rows *named* `:scenes` — and `meshsession:sessions`
**is** this module's scenes row under a different name, carrying the digest-reproduction pattern
verbatim. The ladder excludes by NAME where it meant to exclude by ROLE. It was applied as frozen,
because a ladder rewritten after seeing which row flatters the prediction is no ladder at all; but the
defect is real and the selection was load-bearing (a corrected ladder would have reached
`meshsession:forges`, plausibly a different class).

## does_not_show

That the session laws hold for topologies, episode orders or durations outside those played (two
playthroughs are a corpus, not a universal — L20); wall-clock or throughput over a long session;
recovery from a partition the model does not script; that the enumerated forgeries exhaust the attack
surface. A composed timeline no member contradicts is INTEGRITY across the session, never a claim that
the world it records is true. `integrity ≠ truth`.
